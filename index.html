About

I am a Ph.D. student in Astrophysics at the National Astronomical Observatories, Chinese Academy of Sciences (NAOC), advised by Prof. Jie Wang.

My research explores the role of neutral hydrogen in galaxy formation and evolution. I am particularly interested in how the H I content of galaxies changes across different environments, and how large-scale radio surveys can reveal the distribution and evolution of gas in the nearby Universe.

My current research involves the FAST–M31 survey, the FEASTS survey, and the FAST Hundred-deg² H I Deep (HD²) Survey. I work with observational data processing, source catalog construction, H I mass functions, and the environmental effects on satellite galaxies.

02

Research

Selected research projects in observational radio astronomy and galaxy evolution.

H I Survey · FAST

FAST–M31

Investigating background H I sources in the M31 halo region using FAST observations. My work includes catalog construction and the measurement of the H I mass function using statistical methods.

Galaxy Evolution · FEASTS

FEASTS

Studying the H I properties of satellite galaxies and environmental effects on galaxy evolution, with a particular focus on gas stripping, quenching, and diffuse H I structures.

Large-scale Survey · FAST

HD² Survey

Contributing to the FAST Hundred-deg² H I Deep Survey, including survey planning, observing strategies, HiFAST data processing, and source extraction.

Research Interests

Radio Astronomy

Broader interests include H I scaling relations, the H I mass function, large-scale structure, radio survey techniques, and the connection between gas content and galaxy evolution.
